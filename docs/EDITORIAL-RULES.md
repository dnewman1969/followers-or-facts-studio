# Editorial rules, and how the program enforces them

These come from truth-vlog's roadmap and investigator charter, plus the lessons in
Nimrod's Tower about trusting local AI. "Blocks" means you can't press Approve until
it's fixed.

| Rule | Enforced by |
|------|-------------|
| Knowledge Fight is a research index, never evidence. Credit it on every video. | Research and Script **block** a claim or scene backed only by `kf_index` sources. The credits card and description always carry the credit line with the KF episode numbers. |
| Every on-screen claim resolves to a primary source in the database. | Research **blocks** a claim whose ticked evidence has no primary source (court, government, news, academic...). Script **blocks** a scene that states facts without source IDs, uses a source you didn't tick, or a source ID that doesn't exist. Quote numbers (E14) written where a source goes are read as that quote and its source. |
| The research agents never invent a link. | Links only come from search results, public archives or the database. A found page is kept only if at least one quote from it appears word for word in the saved copy; every link gets a Wayback Machine copy. Story beats that name evidence IDs not in the research are stripped. |
| Be a polite robot. | The agents say who they are, obey robots.txt, wait between requests to the same site, cache every page, and keep under CourtListener's daily limit. |
| The AI never originates a citation or a quote. | Quotes are only kept if they appear word for word in a saved copy of the source. Script **blocks** any quoted text in narration that doesn't match a verified quote. Quote cards are drawn as text, never generated. |
| AI never makes evidence: no fake documents, quotes or footage of real events. | Image prompts always add "no text, no logos, no recognizable real people". Real documents only appear as "receipt" shots from screenshots you saved yourself. |
| Label AI imagery. | Every AI picture and clip is stamped "AI illustration". Uploads are marked as containing altered/synthetic content. |
| Real people appear only as clearly-cartoon caricatures of the public figures who sold the lie (changed 2026-10-23 at Dave's request; was "never draw real people"). | Storyboard **blocks** any picture prompt that names a real person. Caricatures are drawn from a written design you approve in the Cast window (looks only, never the name), only in cartoon satire styles, only of people the database records as saying, spreading, platforming or profiting from the video's claims, never on careful topics, and only in scenes that cite a source about what they did. Each gets a "Caricature: name" caption plus the AI label, and the description says they're satire. No speech bubbles: only verified quotes, as quote cards. Not legal advice; see the report "Creative styles and studio polish". |
| Never draw victims, families, believers or private people. | They can't be added to the Cast; careful topics switch caricatures and humor off. |
| Victims: courts and documents only. | Sandy Hook arcs (and any arc whose notes say "courts only") get `courts_only`, `no_names`, `no_footage` rules. Research **blocks** such a claim without a court or government source; the rules are passed to the script writer and shown as warnings at every stage. |
| Say "unverified" or "contested" plainly. | Research warns; the script writer is told the verdict for each claim. |
| Never mock the believer; aim at the people who sold it. | In the story and script writers' instructions ("anyone can be fooled by this, that's why it works"); humor is a 0-3 setting and is forced to 0 on careful topics; you approve the script. |
| Teach the trick, not just the fact; show the myth once. | Stories name 1-2 manipulation techniques; the script has a "tell" scene (Owlbert's Tell card) and a "toolkit" scene; the writer opens and closes on the fact and shows the myth once, as a claim someone sold. |
| The video is as long as you asked. | Script **blocks** below 80% of the target length (Make it longer rewrites only the short scenes); the storyboard plans enough shots to cover the narration; the render warns if the video comes out short. |
| Every video answers: what was claimed, what the record says, who benefited, what it did to people. | Script **blocks** if any of the four isn't marked on a scene. |
| Plain 6th-grade language. | Script shows the reading level and warns above grade 8. |
| Corrections happen in public. | Description and pinned comment invite corrections. |
| Same-model self-critique isn't verification; a human approves. | Checks are plain text matching, not AI opinions. Nothing advances without your Approve. |
| Failures surface; nothing is silently swapped. | A failed job pauses the queue. No stock-photo or canned-audio fallbacks. Every approval and failure is kept in the stage history. |
| Real pictures only with a licence we can prove (added 2026-10-26 at Dave's request). | The storyboard may ask for a "real" shot (a place, object, document, event or public figure the narration names). `mediafinder.py` searches Wikimedia Commons, then Openverse (commercial filter), and accepts only public domain, CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA; non-commercial, no-derivatives, unknown and news-agency pictures (Getty, AP, Reuters, AFP, Shutterstock...) are refused. Never real pictures of victims or private people, none of people or the event on careful topics, and a public figure only when the research links them to selling the claim. Every picture keeps a provenance record, a credit on screen and a line under "Image credits" in the description. Real photos are never animated by AI (2.5D moves only). |
| Re-creations are labelled (added 2026-10-26 at Dave's request; replaces "no AI footage of real events" for illustrations only). | When no usable real picture exists, the shot is painted as a drawn re-creation (never photo-real people; the usual "no recognizable real people" guard), stamped RE-CREATION on screen and in clips, and the description says scenes marked RE-CREATION are illustrations, not footage. AI still never makes documents, quotes or footage presented as real. |
| Writers may look things up, but finds are evidence like any other. | Script and book planners list questions the research can't answer; "Look these up" sends them to the research agents (hidden stage `lookup`); finds arrive on the Research page NEW and unticked, and nothing reaches a script or book until you tick it. |
| No broken record. | Each verified quote is used in one scene; every scene and chapter is told what was already said; sentences that re-tell an earlier scene go back to the writer, then show as warnings (Fix repeats); book paragraphs that re-tell earlier chapters are removed and listed in the review notes. |
| Story, not report; the facts stay fixed (added 2026-10-27 at Dave's request). | Before any scene is written the planner makes a film treatment (`stages/treatment.py`): title, logline, opening scene, three acts, key characters, major revelations, emotional arc, open questions, closing thought. Characters must be real people or groups named in the research (or plain groups like "the believers"); any invented name is dropped and logged. Every revelation must name ticked evidence or it is dropped. The director is described by traits only, never named. Report language ("according to the research", "in summary", "this report"...) counts as stock phrasing and goes back to the writer. |
| The Virtual Director decides how it's shown, never what happened (added 2026-10-28 at Dave's request). | Between the shot plan and the painting, `director.py` scores every shot against the ones before it (N-1 to N-5, N-10) with plain counting, not an AI opinion: 90%+ alike = reject, 70-89% = repair, plus camera, pose, place, light, colour and pacing rules. Repairs change only camera, composition, light, move, pose and mood. A rewrite that names a real person, goes to a place the scene never mentions, or flips day to night inside a scene is turned down. Your own shot ideas are scored but never rewritten. A shot still rejected blocks approval. Careful topics get sober framing only (no tilted angles, neon, pastel or handheld). |
| Claims that don't hold up stay out (added 2026-10-28 at Dave's request). | A ticked claim reaches stories, script and cast only if it holds up: a primary record (from the research database, or with a word-for-word verified quote), or two independent sources with verified quotes (different website and publisher; near-copies such as wire stories count once), or two independent sources from your own research database (you checked those). The deep dive never tries to prove the lie true (the people who sold it are known liars): it checks attribution (when and where they said or sold it) and the record (what courts, regulators or experts found, who profited). Its finds arrive unticked; one button ticks them all after you've looked. A claim whose own verdict is unverified or contested doesn't hold up. Weak claims get a Deep dive (Research page): who/what/when/where questions, primary records first; finds arrive unticked. Still weak: left out, logged, never called false. |
| Every fact sentence is backed by its scene's own evidence (added 2026-10-28). | After writing, a different model than the writer checks each sentence that states a fact against that scene's quotes and sources, and takes out what isn't backed. This check can only remove, never approve or add; the word-for-word quote match stays the hard gate and your Approve stays final. Everything removed is in claim-check.json (a record of good faith). |
| Each quote once; repeats are deleted, not rewritten (added 2026-10-28). | A scene sees only the quotes the fact budget gave it. A verified quote stays in the first scene that uses it; a later sentence carrying it, or re-telling an earlier scene, is taken out. The length follows the ticked research: short material makes a shorter, honest video, never a padded one. |
| A victim's or a child's likeness is never used (Dave, 2026-10-28). | Public figures who sold the claim may be caricatured (satire and commentary on public figures). Victims, children, families, witnesses and private people never appear in any form: no caricature, no real photo, no re-creation of their likeness. |
| Caricatures are designed from several photos, never one (added 2026-10-28; changed the same day at Dave's request). | Cast → Research looks from photos: a normal web image search (plus Wikipedia and Commons) for photos of the public figure, only to LOOK at, like a cartoonist does. The local vision model answers multiple-choice questions about the face in 3-6 photos with a clear face; only what most photos agree on is kept, and only 1-3 recognisable features are pushed. The photos are deleted straight after: never used in a video, never sent to the picture maker, no face measurements or face ID. Never skin, weight, disability, age or anything tied to race, ethnicity or religion: there are no fields for them. Exaggerate the act and a signature prop, not the body. You approve every design. |
| No brand, show or artist names leave the app (added 2026-10-28). | Looks are described by their traits. Brand, show, studio and format names and the artists behind a look (data/watchlist.json plus every "inspired by" note) are stripped from picture prompts and block titles and tags. Picture prompts say only what to draw (the turbo image model can't use "no ..."). |
| A real person's name never reaches the picture maker (added 2026-10-29). | A shot idea that names a real person becomes that person's approved caricature when the scene cites what they did and the look is a drawn cartoon one (comic, cartoon explainer, courtroom sketch, noir, satire and the drawn new looks); otherwise the name is replaced by an anonymous figure seen from behind. A last guard strips any name left in the finished prompt. |
| An approved caricature belongs to the person, not the video (added 2026-10-29). | Approving a design keeps it with the person's database record: the design, the style it was approved in, the preview you picked (copied to `caricatures/<person>/approved.png` in the data folder) and the video it was approved in. Any later video about them starts with it already approved and, unless you pick another caricature style, draws them in the same style. Every other rule still applies in each video: only if they sold this video's claims, never on careful topics, only in scenes that cite what they did. Untick Approved to retire it everywhere. |
| Search tags are honest (added 2026-10-30). | The write-up fills YouTube's 500 characters of tags with what people really search for about THIS video: its people, claims and subject, plus the channel's tags. The local AI is told no other channels, shows, brands or creators and no clickbait the video can't back up; brand, show and artist names are taken out and the write-up check blocks any that remain. Misleading tags count as spam on YouTube. |
| Story ideas use only the research (added 2026-10-30). | Scan data for stories keeps only ideas whose claims and evidence are in the ticked research; the stories, clips or book made from an idea are told to stay on its evidence, and every story check (claim, record, benefit, harm, verdict) still applies, info clips included. Lengths come from the material, never padded. |
| Clearing research never deletes the original database (added 2026-10-30). | Clear all and Delete remove a video's own files and, only if you tick the box, the sources the research agents found. The research database you imported (stories, claims, people, sources) stays, and anything already on YouTube stays there. |
